# Limitations

Output is compared with images rendered by Windows. This page describes the current rendering behaviour and known differences from native Windows playback. The exact per-fixture bounds and tolerance levels are defined in `src/gdi-parity.fixture.test.ts`.

Parity is measured by comparing rendered output pixel-for-pixel against images captured from Windows GDI and GDI+. Each case in `src/gdi-parity.fixture.test.ts` carries a single mismatch tolerance bound, many of them nonzero to allow for fixed-point rounding and platform differences. The report script `bun scripts/gdi-fixtures/report.ts` reports zero-tolerance differences separately. See [the current parity priorities](./outstanding-work.md#current-parity-priorities-8-october-2026) for the zero-tolerance review, text coverage gaps and reference-environment issues.

## Color adjustment and image effects

- The `HALFTONE` stretch mode matches the existing uniform-axis controls for 32-bit output, but Windows takes a second, filtered enlargement branch for some sources. Which branch is a rule of the source rectangle alone (never the destination size, which the 2x and 3x controls vary, the colour adjustment or the blit API) found with boundary sweeps against the native engine (`src/emf-gdi-halftone-branch.ts`, `halftone-boundary.fixture.test.ts`): the engine counts distinct colours while scanning the rows and compares the count with limits that depend on the rectangle's size. A colour is the exact 24-bit value, except that colours whose red equals blue are identified by the top six bits of blue and green. Up to 2,304 pixels the branch is always replicated; above 16,384 pixels only rows 0, 6, 12, ... are looked at and 20 distinct colours filter; in between every row is scanned, 145 + floor(o / 16) colours filter (o is how far the least row count with more than 2,304 pixels overshoots 2,304), and so do 20 colours with more than 2,304 pixels in rows that introduced a colour, unless duplicate rows first use up a budget. The rule agrees with every one of the 29,763 swept native sources, with all 242 captured 2x sources and with the single-pixel swap that flips a 256 x 16 band source from replicated to filtered. A source whose height is exactly the least row count past 2,304 pixels (8,766 swept sources, 4,500 of them drawn after the rule for that height was found) needs 289 + floor(o / 8) colours or the 20-colour rule, and the scan gives up as soon as the colours counted plus one row of pixels for every duplicate row reach that limit minus one: one duplicate row before the last colours that are needed already ends it when the rows are wide, while rows that each add only a few colours (2 or 5 per row of a 100 x 24 image) never use the budget and filter at the last row. The filtered branch is reproduced for: enlargements of both axes by up to 5x, whole or fractional (the source is sharpened with the four-neighbour kernel and interpolated vertical first with an 8-bit intermediate rounded half up, a replicated and separately sharpened row beyond the top and bottom; each destination pixel belongs to the source pixel under its centre, the destination pixels of one source pixel form a run, and the three weights come from the run's length and the pixel's place in it: a lone pixel `(5,22,5)/32`, a pair `(4,12,0)` and `(0,12,4)` over 16, a run of three `(6,10,0), (1,14,1), (0,10,6)`, four `(6,10,0), (3,12,1), (1,12,3), (0,10,6)` and five `(13,19,0), (6,25,1), (3,26,3), (1,25,6), (0,19,13)` over 32, so a whole factor, 1.5x and 1.025x all draw on the same five sets); either axis enlarged by more than 5x, even by a fraction (horizontal first, the sharpened rows clamped at the edges, the 13-bit shares of the kernel table described below, with measured exact shares in place of the table for the whole factors 9, 10, 11, 13, 15 and 16); one axis enlarged while the other keeps its size (only that axis is sharpened, by a one-dimensional pass `v + floor((2v - l - r) / 4)`, and interpolated with the same table, an exact 2x being linear; exact from 1.5x to 10x, three channel values one level off at 1.025x); mirrored blits (the mirror image of the unmirrored result); and a combined (dithered) colour adjustment (the source and its extension rows are dithered with the ordered pattern and mapped first; for whole factors up to 5x the pattern runs two rows further down than for the replicated branch, and two rows less far when mirrored vertically). All 240 selection controls, the 80 native 2x captures, 37 of the 48 cases of `halftone-filtered` and the one-axis profiles of every phase of every whole factor from 2 to 16 on both axes are pixel-exact; the dithered cases keep 0.15% to 0.3% of their channel values one or two levels off (the colour-adjustment residual the replicated branch has too). Not reproduced: filtered mixed enlarge-and-reduce stretches and a dithered adjustment on a stretch that keeps one axis; these still replicate. The replicated branch first smooths exact two-by-two checkers, then takes the nearest source pixel. A reduction averages each pixel's footprint and then sharpens. All seven plain uniform-axis fixtures (2x, 3x, 0.5x, 0.65x and 1.37x) match exactly. Mixed-axis stretching follows the native reduce, per-axis sharpen, then enlarge sequence; all 96 native size/pattern combinations match byte for byte (`src/halftone-mixed.fixture.test.ts`). The reduction keeps its weights through the sharpening: each footprint is split into 8192 shares, and a source pixel takes the difference of the cumulative shares `floor(j * 8192 / footprint)` at its edges (exact for the native impulse and random-data captures of over 100 size pairs, and for sources up to 300 pixels), and an enlargement other than exactly 2x area-resamples the sharpened samples to destination size and smooths that row with a destination-space FIR that spans one source pixel either way: its taps sample one box-plus-triangle-like kernel (about a quarter box, the rest a triangle with a bent, quadratic-looking tail) at `k / ratio` source pixels and are normalised to 1. The native weights are whole 13-bit shares: the cumulative weight of a destination pixel up to source pixel `j` is rounded up, a pixel's weight is the difference of two cumulative shares, and a row sums to exactly 8192 (impulse- and random-data-fitted integer weight rows of about 410 size pairs, ratios 1.05x to 8x, have this structure, and a mirrored enlarged axis reverses the finished rows instead of the source). The kernel is an interpolation table (steps of 1/96 source pixel) solved for the one curve that reproduces all those measured rows; its generating formula is not known, so it is a fit: sizes that were held out of the fit get about 95% of their weight rows exact and the rest are one share off between two neighbouring source pixels (random 8-24 pixel images with an enlarged and a halved-or-more reduced axis, 436 of them against native: 0.02% of the bytes differ and 94% of the images are exact, where the previous float kernel differed in 1.7% of the bytes and matched 6%). A pair of rows (columns) squeezed into one while the other axis grows is despeckled first (a pixel of a one-pixel 2 x 2 checker block becomes the rounded mean of its pair) and, when the other axis at least doubles, keeps only its last row (column), which is then enlarged like any other single-row source; this closes every single-row destination in the captures. Native ramp/checker fixtures retain colour and edge differences there (bounds in the parity tests). The horizontal ramp enlargement is within one level everywhere.
- `EMR_SETCOLORADJUSTMENT` affects `HALFTONE` `StretchBlt` and `StretchDIBits` calls, as in GDI. The stages, their order and constants were measured on native 32x32x32 colour cubes: per-channel gamma, reference black/white, contrast (`exp(0.0148885 c)`), brightness (`0.95625 b`), log and negative curves match every input level exactly. Illuminants 1-5, 7 and 8 use the native colour cubes; colorfulness and tint scale and turn CIE u'v' chroma exactly (1 + c/100, 0.6 degrees per tint unit) and compress out-of-gamut colours affinely (after flooring the turned u' at 0, which the strongly rotated blues need), with a remaining +-1 rounding difference on about 0.05-0.4% of the channels (the BT.709 matrix is the 7-decimal one derived from the primaries and D65 white; a colour adjustment that combines an illuminant with colorfulness or tint feeds the illuminant's unclamped linear output into the chroma stage, which is within a level on most channels but still up to 70 levels off for a few strongly blue colours). Illuminant, colorfulness and tint go through Windows' 32-level palette with its 66x65 ordered dither, reproduced from native captures including the pattern origin, so the illuminant controls and the combined-adjustment fixtures are pixel-exact apart from that chroma rounding (at most 3 levels) and, for mixed-axis stretches of non-2x ratios, the geometry bounds below. Native captures cover mirrored enlargement, brush origins and rotated/skewed blits; rotated adjustment follows the first HALFTONE blit in the playback session. Direct-DIB colour-band transitions remain open (see the parity priorities).
- EMF+ image effects (`SerializableObject`) are applied to the image's pixels before it is drawn. MS-EMFPLUS does not specify the algorithms, so they were measured against the effected bitmaps GDI+ itself writes into its recordings (`src/emf-plus-image-effects.fixture.test.ts`). Color matrix, lookup table, brightness/contrast and color balance match the native fixtures exactly. Sharpen strength matches all integer amounts 0–100 independently of radius. Blur: every filter product is kept to 1/256 of a level, the centre weight is applied as two half-weight products that are each rounded on their own, and each sum rounds half up. Below radius 20 GDI+ filters each row horizontally, then vertically filters the leading ceil(height / width) rows, and the converter follows that rule; the native ramp and colour-impulse dimension sweeps are exact. At radius 20 and above, native reduction factors are measured across 957 quarter radii, and the native algorithm is reproduced axis by axis: every row is reduced horizontally (floor of the block mean, a partial last block averaged over its own pixels), filtered at the reduced radius, and enlarged back to full width (linear, truncating, continuing the end line beyond the outermost samples; a single reduced sample leaves one trailing pixel unfiltered); every column of those rows then goes through the same reduce, filter and enlarge. A kernel whose half-width, rounded up to odd, reaches the reduced size (partial block included) clamps at the ends, a narrower one reflects. The kernel weights are float32 values formed in arithmetic that truncates toward zero (the offset accumulates as `q_i = q_(i-1) + 1.4 / radius`, `w_i = exp(-q_i^2)`, the sum is `w_0 + 2 w_1 + 2 w_2 + ...` in order, and each weight is `w_i * (256 / sum)`), which decides every product that lands within a few ten-thousandths of a rounding tie. With them, blur is exact: no pixel differs from GDI+ across random noise images at random radii from 1 to 255 and sizes from 8 to 512 pixels, nor in the ramp, impulse, 957-radius and two-pixel-wide captures, and so is sharpen. Expanded blur reduces a cropped source rectangle from its own corner and enlarges only that rectangle's own reduced samples (the transparent padding samples shape the filtering alone), with samples beyond the buffer transparent rather than reflected; a lone reduced sample is continued toward a transparent neighbour from its block's middle, or from just past the last pixel of a shorter block. The 504 expanded draws and a further 150 narrow and edge-reaching draws are exact. Color curves use complete native lookups for all eight adjustments and every legal intensity, matching all 256 levels. Levels also handles inverted and equal thresholds and the full midtone range; its tables are formed in float32 arithmetic that truncates toward zero and match a 258,560-value sweep and 2.6 million further native values exactly. Hue/saturation/lightness is exact: GDI+ works in all-integer HSL (lightness `(max+min)>>1`, saturation floored to 0-255, a 255-step hue, 16.16 fixed-point controls) and the converter reproduces it for the whole 16.7 million-colour RGB cube at 20 settings and the 1,390,080-pair sweep. Tint is exact: GDI+ works in integer luma-preserving arithmetic (luma weights 54/183/19 over 256, the tint colour from the 256-index hue palette with a maximum of 254, the amount as a signed weight `round(2.55 |amount|)` that scales the largest channel by `w / 256`) and the converter reproduces it for the whole 16.7 million-colour RGB cube at every amount and 20 hues, and the 253,440-pair sweep. Red-eye correction follows a model measured from GDI+'s outputs alone (a clean-room, black-box fit; see the header of `src/emf-plus-image-effects.ts`): per area, redness `R - max(G, B)` is pulled toward grey in 60 six-degree sectors around a centroid of weights `redness / L`, where `L = round((9 G + 2 B) / 11)` is an integer luma (red plays no part; the weight is 1 when `L = 0`). Two classes of area of more than one red pixel use the previous weights instead (redness over `G + 0.225 B`, 1 / 1.225 below 1, 0.81 of the previous centroid carried): areas holding a red pixel of luma 0 and scenes whose centroid lies exactly on a pixel centre; the luma weights measured worse there, and using them would have raised eight of the per-capture residual ceilings in `src/emf-plus-redeye.fixture.test.ts`, none of which was raised. The previous area's centroid, in pixel-index coordinates and before any fallback, is added once to the next area's weighted sums (an area without red passes it on, a red one chains it), and a centroid farther from the area's middle than `(w + h) / 6` is replaced by the middle. Each sector's mean redness sets the correction through a quadratic falloff whose strength steps from 1/4 to 1/2, 0.661 and 3/4 when the area's 30th-to-70th-percentile luma spread exceeds 25, 40 and 80; a second term `(1 - D / Dm)^2 / 2` with the darkness `D = G + 2/9 B` corrects pixels much darker than their sector (pupils) and is used below twice the sector mean. The one brightest pixel (its luma strictly above every other pixel's and at least 40 above the 70th percentile) is corrected completely, and no pixel near it keeps more than five levels of redness per squared pixel of distance. Output is `R -= round(11/16 a)`, `G, B += round(5/16 a)`. The share of mismatching pixels on the native fixtures is 0.057/0.324/1.81% (left/both/whole; 12/68/380 pixels, down from 12/99/582), mostly in the colour-chart draw whose pure-red bands the model cannot reach. The stage captures (`redeye-stages`, `src/emf-plus-redeye-stages.fixture.test.ts`) pin the rules one at a time: all 412 lone red pixels inside the fallback circle are exact, as are 118 of 120 centroid-weight captures and 13 of 14 carry captures, 28 of 30 strength steps are exact at the centre pixel and 47 of 81 highlight captures are exact over the whole area (the symmetric ones use the previous weights). 195 of 240 independent and held-out controls with non-zero blue are exact (206 of 264 with the pure-red ones, from 103 and 114). Not reproduced: areas that hold red pixels of luma 0 (pure red, or green and blue under 3), whose native strength is the one the calls before it left behind (an all-pure-red area gets four different strengths depending on the earlier calls, and the earlier call must be 20 x 20 pixels or larger to reach it; see the stage captures), the sector of the pixels exactly on the four axes of a symmetric scene whose centroid is a pixel centre, the exact centre of lone pixels beyond the fallback circle (138 of 709 stage captures one level off), and one-level rounding at ties in the output.
- EmfPlusDual recordings add a processed bitmap fallback after the effect draw. EmfPlusOnly recordings carry the effect and original source without that fallback. Both types have been regenerated and tested: 74 cases each, plus an explicit Dual blur case. Only recordings test the effect algorithms directly; red-eye retains measured residuals. Deferred PNG and SVG draws preserve their effects and cropped source mapping.
- An effect is applied to the draw's source rectangle only, reading one more column and row, as GDI+ does. A blur with `expandEdge` blurs transparency in at the edges but, as in GDI+, draws nothing beyond the source rectangle. MS-EMFPLUS requires pixel source units; malformed non-pixel records do not apply effects.
- GDI+ records a color curve effect under the lookup table's identifier, and its own playback then draws the image without the effect. The converter does the same.
- An effect is skipped when the image cannot be decoded to pixels. PNG, BMP, JPEG, GIF and TIFF have bundled decoders. Animated GIF and multipage TIFF initially draw their first frame/page, as GDI+ does. Native references verify offset GIF frames, background colours, transparency, local palettes, LZW-compressed and multipage TIFF, plus odd-width bilevel uncompressed, PackBits and CCITT Group 3/4 TIFF. Group 3 also handles reversed bit order and multiple strips. Deflate strips (both compression tags and horizontal prediction) and uncompressed/Deflate tiles also match native references exactly. JPEG-compressed TIFF strips and tiles (RGB and YCbCr) and standalone JPEG (greyscale, RGB, 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:2:0, progressive) match the native references exactly: the bundled decoder reproduces libjpeg's accurate integer IDCT, triangle chroma upsampling and fixed-point colour conversion. Arithmetic-coded JPEG is decoded by the bundled decoder and matches GDI+ exactly on the libjpeg-turbo sample and on 23 encoder-made native captures (`scripts/gdi-fixtures/arith-jpeg-encoder.ts`, a port of libjpeg's `jcarith.c`): sequential and progressive (spectral selection with and without successive approximation), restart intervals, custom DAC conditioning (including extreme L/U/K values), 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:4:0 and 4:2:0 sampling, greyscale, and two images (the 37 x 19 pattern and a 61 x 45 high-magnitude noise image). GDI+ refuses none of these variants. Arithmetic CMYK and YCCK (sequential, progressive, restarts) decode to the same pixels as a Huffman copy of the same coefficients and go through the CMYK conversion described below. A 12-bit JPEG cannot be decoded by Windows either ("Unsupported JPEG data precision 12"), and GDI+ draws nothing for it; the converter does the same, in PNG and SVG output. CMYK and YCCK JPEG (Adobe transform 0 or 2, or no Adobe marker, whose samples are always read as inverted) are converted the way Windows does it. GDI+ sends them through the Windows ICM colour-management module (`mscms.dll`, the RSWOP CMYK profile to sRGB, perceptual intent, best-quality transform: byte for byte what `TranslateBitmapBits` returns), which resamples the profile onto a 16 x 16 x 16 x 16 table of 16-bit integer colours, interpolates it tetrahedrally (the four fractions sorted in descending order) and keeps the top eight bits; nodes beyond the sRGB gamut are not clipped. Where an ink sits on the grid is the profile's own input curve: the A2B0 tag of RSWOP.icm carries a 256-entry table per ink (the same four times), `t(0) = 0`, `t(v) = 256 v - 1` up to `v = 224`, then a steeper line to `t(255) = 65535`, and the module puts node `j` at `4369 j` (`65535 / 15`), so ink `v` is at grid position `t(v) / 4369` and `v = 255` reaches the last node (`cmykInputCurve` in `src/jpeg-cmyk.ts`, checked against all 1,024 table entries by `generate-cmyk-lut.ts curve`; the earlier model that took the sample as `v << 8` was 1/4369 of a cell off for every ink up to 224, up to 0.058 of a cell off above it and could not reach the last node). `TranslateColors` accepts 16-bit ink words (a `COLOR` record is 16 bytes in a 64-bit process, 8 in a 32-bit one) and maps a word through that table at index `word / 257`; for `257 v` it returns exactly the 16-bit colour that `TranslateBitmapBits` produces for the 8-bit ink `v` (6,000 of 6,000 words in `icm-cmyk-translate16.bin`), so the 8-bit path is the 16-bit one before it keeps the top byte. `src/jpeg-cmyk.ts` bundles the table (about 196 KB of text, solved from 6,000,000 `mscms.dll` samples by `scripts/gdi-fixtures/generate-cmyk-lut.ts`: a least-squares fit, then each node moved in steps of 4 levels to minimise the squared error of the samples it touches, and the 226 nodes that pure inks, equal-ink mixtures and neutral ramps interpolate between refined to single levels; the RSWOP profile itself, which Windows ships, is not bundled). On the committed 8,000 held-out ink combinations 99.80% of the channel values are exact (23,951 of 24,000, none off by more than one level), against 99.27% (23,824) before the input curve was identified; on the committed references the CMYK patch files are all exact, the photos 99.74% to 99.80%, the ramps 99.77% and the YCCK patches 99.74%, with no value more than one level away; on the 1,000,000 random ink combinations that `generate-cmyk-lut.ts fit` holds out (a measurement the script reports, not a committed capture) 99.742% are exact against 99.349% before. CMYK JPEG inside a TIFF (PhotometricInterpretation 5) goes through the same transform with the ink amounts read as stored, not inverted (96 x 64 reference: 99.74% exact, largest difference 1). Chroma subsampling and Adobe/no-Adobe variants behave like the exact decoder; the earlier `jpeg-js` fallback matched 1% to 11% of CMYK channel values with errors up to 135, and the 17^4 grid fitted to GDI+ output before the transform was identified matched 87% to 95% with errors up to 6. Canvas backends are bypassed for these families, so `@napi-rs/canvas` and browsers cannot change the result; in SVG output such files are embedded as decoded pixels rather than as the original JPEG. A canvas backend remains preferred when available. Other encoded formats may need a browser or `@napi-rs/canvas`.

## Pen transforms

EMF+ pen transforms (uniform, non-uniform and skewed) match GDI+ exactly on the 17 pen-transform fixtures. That includes dashes, which GDI+ lays out along the path in world space at the untransformed pen width. Native fixtures verify dashes under uniform and non-uniform scales, including antialiasing. GDI+ rejects singular pen transforms; the converter ignores such malformed transform data. Square, round, diamond and arrow anchor caps use their native shapes; the new centered-cap fixtures match every pixel with and without antialiasing.

Clockwise inside-frame RoundRects round vertical inset widths up and curve controls up, with horizontal half-FIX shifts applied in both arc directions. `emf-insideframe-scaled` and `wmf-shapes-scaled` are now pixel-exact. In `GM_ADVANCED` an Arc, Chord or Pie under an inside-frame pen wider than its box paints nothing and has an empty path (as measured with a DIB probe and `GetPath`), where an Ellipse, Rectangle or RoundRect keeps the box as given. 2,400 native `GetPath` captures at fractional scales (`emf-scaled-paths.json.gz`) match every shape, counter-clockwise and clockwise, point for point. A pen exactly as wide as the box leaves a zero-size frame: an Ellipse, Rectangle and RoundRect still match (the corner geometry is scaled onto it; one RoundRect is a FIX off), while Windows builds an Arc, Chord or Pie there from rounding noise, which is not reproduced. In `GM_COMPATIBLE` (WMF) a pen wider than an Ellipse or RoundRect leaves the box without the pen's inset.

## Text

Grayscale GDI+ glyph origins now snap independently to the nearest quarter pixel in x and y, verified on native sweeps at 1/64-pixel intervals. ClearType snaps horizontal placement to the nearest sixth pixel and preserves that fraction through scan conversion, while vertical placement snaps to whole pixels. Diagonal/base glyph hinting still differs.

- The ANSI, small-text and multi-string record fixtures retain glyph-edge differences under 0.1% (`src/emf-text-records.fixture.test.ts`). PolyTextOut C1 handling now follows native playback; focused Courier controls are exact and Arial retains three edge pixels. EMF+ masks retain decorations and use native grayscale hinting and 16 shades; 150 grayscale and 34 ClearType baseline-controlled glyph captures, plus every captured contrast mapping, are exact. Broader ClearType, antialiasing and TextContrast fixtures still differ, including diagonal hinting and fractional positioning. Current fonts reproduce the three `textx-plus` antialias/ClearType references exactly, so those residuals are renderer differences; other old captures lack complete provenance.
- ANSI text is decoded by the LOGFONT charset: ANSI and DEFAULT as Windows-1252 (Windows would use the system ANSI code page for DEFAULT), Mac, Shift-JIS, Hangul, GB2312, Big5, Greek, Turkish, Vietnamese, Hebrew, Arabic, Baltic, Russian, Thai and East European through the host's `TextDecoder` with the matching Windows code page, Johab and OEM from bundled Windows tables (OEM assumes code page 437; Windows uses the system OEM code page), Symbol as raw byte codes. Unknown charset values fall back to Windows-1252.
- Vertical `ETO_PDY` advances are supported with or without the `fonts` option.
- Without `fonts`, SVG text measurements are estimates. Supply the matching fonts for exact justification.

## Wide pens and paths

- All 2,136 paths in the native `WidenPath` sweep (2–64 px pens, every cap and join) fill identically. The flattened-pen perpendicular follows a closed rule instead of a fitted table: the interpolated point of a pen edge, rounded to the half-pixel grid after a half-unit bias, with a further half unit when the edge's extent in that channel is odd. It matches native `WidenPath` for every direction tested at integer, fractional and wider-than-100 px widths (about 390,000 whole-pixel directions at widths from 1 to 206 px, none differing), including vertical segments, exact ties and segments parallel to a pen edge.
- Wide pens on curves (Beziers, Arcs, Chords, Pies, AngleArcs and paths with curves) follow the pen at each flattened segment instead of treating the curve as a polyline: a pure Bezier matches native `WidenPath` exactly under every cap and join in 80 captured cases (previously none). Arcs, chords and pies now match native `WidenPath` in all 240 captured outlines (previously 92; `curve-widen.json`, differing pixels 1,437 to 0; 239 of 240 before round 4): at one logical unit per device pixel (other scales cut it to whole logical units, as dashes do: a dashed pen under a world scale or anisotropic map mode lays its pattern out in logical units and measures each segment by its logical vector rounded to the nearest whole unit; `scaled-dash-caps.json.gz`: 1,416 native dashed square-capped arcs under six scales are all fill-identical, 95 before) a segment's square-cap extension is its vector cut down to whole pixels (components shifted right by four, the dash measure), a curve end uses its end tangent that way and a curve cut to one segment its chord, a line meeting a curve end takes the curve's tangent perpendicular, and the join inside one flattened cubic keeps its own pen vertices. `arc-cap-sweep.json.gz` (1,436 square-capped arcs swept over the end angle, two widths and start angles) is exact vertex for vertex. All 80 arcs, 80 pies and 80 chords are exact (round 4: the last chord, sample 206, was not the widener: the native `GetPath` of that chord widens exactly, and the arc geometry started a FIX off at a rounding tie, a start point at y = 3627.49993 FIX where native rounds up; 5,820 of 5,841 native widened near-complete chords fill identically from their own paths, the 21 others have a closing line under 8 FIX, `chord-closing.json.gz`). The arc points are now computed in single precision the way native does (float32 trigonometry nodes, float32 radius times unit coordinate): 900 of 900 native counter-clockwise arcs and 187 of 260 on the large circles are exact (898 and 182 before), and 168 of the 169 chords around sample 206 equal native (108 of them started a FIX off before). A chord whose arc flattens to a single segment is a closed polygon of two vertices that the closing line retraces: it takes joins at both vertices and no caps (before, it was widened as an open polyline with caps; all 1,044 native chords on arcs of 1 to 15 degrees, two widths, every cap and join, now fill identically, `chord-sweep.json.gz`, where 528 differed before). Flat-capped round joins match 5,998 of 5,999 native random three-point paths (previously 86 differed), and fractional pen widths 6.5–44 px match 2,997 of 3,000 random paths (previously 286 differed).
