## EMF+ rotated high-quality DrawImage

A rotated or sheared `HighQualityBicubic`/`HighQualityBilinear` `DrawImage` (a 3-point destination) is modelled from native draws of solid, noise, impulse and transparent-bordered images (`System.Drawing`, public API) over angles 5 to 70 degrees and scales 0.4 to 6 (`gpx-image-rotated-highqualitybicubic` 0.142% before, 0.013% now, two pixels over 8 levels):

- If either source axis is unscaled (its device length rounded up is within one pixel of the source length: 20 texels at scales 0.905 to 1.05), GDI+ point-samples with the plain Bicubic (`a = -0.5`) or Bilinear kernel, with no half-pixel shift.
- Otherwise the area-integrated kernel applies. Under PixelOffsetMode Half/HighQuality the sample grid sits half a device pixel further along each of the image's own axes (coverage unchanged; impulse responses match within 4 levels, where they were 3,500 pixels off before on noise).
- The far (right and bottom) edges fade: native alpha is the kernel's alpha minus a share of the alpha the kernel has at the edge, zero at the edge and rejoining the kernel one device pixel in, independent of angle and scale (cubic table in `fadeAt`; linear for the tent). The earlier "15 to 17 levels low over the last 0.3 pixels" was this fade plus the shift.
- A tap outside the source rectangle but inside the bitmap reads the bitmap (every interpolation mode, measured on sub-rectangle draws); only taps outside the bitmap are transparent. The previous transparent-outside-the-rectangle rule held only for whole-bitmap draws.

Remaining: the rotated interior colour differs by up to about 9 levels on noise at upscales (the impulse response itself is within 4), `HighQualityBilinear` is within 3 to 4 levels of the tent model at 2x to 5x, an upscale between 1.1x and 2x still has the largest noise residual, and the far-edge fade table is measured at scales of 2 or more (a smaller scale does not follow it as closely). ## EMF+ gradients

Path gradients with uniform surrounds are closed to the step and, but for pixels exactly on a copy's edge, to the pixel; ones with per-vertex surround colours are Gouraud fan triangles. GDI+ draws a path gradient as `N = ceil(2 * hypot(w, h))` nested copies of its boundary (`w` x `h` is the device-space bounding box: a 600 x 80 rectangle has 1,211 steps, 2,422 under a world or brush scale 2, and a diagonal that is a whole number of quarter pixels is not rounded past itself), each a step closer to the centre colour, and a pixel takes the colour of the innermost copy that holds it. `pathGradientQuantum` and `quantisedPathArgb` (`emf-plus-brush-gradient.ts`) rebuild that: the ratio rounds to a whole step, the outer half counts steps from the boundary colour and the inner half from the centre colour (they round a level apart at half-way), premultiplied channels and alpha interpolate together, and Blend and InterpolationColors curves are read at the stepped position. The sampler in `emf-plus-exact-fill.ts` passes the step count; the nudge that selects a left-inclusive span no longer moves the sampled ratio.

How it was found: the probe in `PathGradientStepProbe.cs` captures whole rows through white-to-black rectangles, where the path-gradient geometry is trivial (a row is the plane `255 x / half-width` up to rounding). 97% of values matched round(255 t); the remainder looked like noise until it was seen to depend on the rectangle's HEIGHT (rows of identical width and different height fall into groups whose boundaries sit where `floor(4 * L)` changes, `L` the half diagonal). Left and right of the centre mirror exactly; the left half is `round(255 k / N)` and the right half `255 - round(255 k / N)` for the step count `k = round(t N)`. The step count follows the half diagonal of the bounding box for rectangles of every size and centre, for triangles and ellipses (centred or not), under world/brush scales (device-space bounds), with Blend and preset curves read at `k / N`, and for isotropic focus (contours scaled about the centre). 72 rows reach 25,390 of 25,568 pixels; the 64 images 99.25-100%; the earlier 120 colour, 240 alpha, 252 focus-contour captures rise by 98,306, 20,132 (translucent), 35,628 (uniform alpha) and 18,270 exact pixels (see limitations.md for the totals), with every per-capture ceiling in `path-gradient-color-bounds.json` tightened (176 of 360 entries) and none loosened.

Per-vertex surround colours are not nested copies at all. GDI+ fans the boundary into triangles (centre, vertex i, vertex i + 1) and Gouraud-shades each with a whole-pixel scan converter; `emf-plus-path-gradient-vertices.ts` reproduces it. Found by isolating one channel at a time (a red-only vertex makes each channel the weight map of one vertex; the three channels of an RGB triangle are independent and identical to the three single-channel captures) and then reading how the colour behaves along one scanline of one fan triangle:
(1) a span runs from `ceil(xLeft)` to `ceil(xRight)` at the integer row, with no sub-pixel correction, so the colour at the first pixel is the left edge's colour rather than the colour at the crossing (this alone is the x shift of about -0.5 pixel that a plane fit to the colour shows, and it is why a smooth fan model is up to four levels off on slanted edges but not on a horizontal one); the right edge's colour belongs to `ceil(xRight)`, the first pixel past the span, so the per-pixel step is the end difference over `xe - xs`, not over the true width. (2) Each edge is walked along its major axis (x when |dx| > |dy|) in `trunc(|d|)` whole steps from `floor` of its first vertex, taking the edges in the order centre -> vertex i, vertex i -> vertex i + 1, vertex i + 1 -> centre; the edge parameter at a column or row `u` is `|u - floor(a)| / trunc(|b - a|)` (the colour at `x = 32` on one edge was `k / 11` for six rows, with the denominator `trunc(11.69)`; rows give `k / 13` for a 13.75-row edge). (3) The span interpolates in 16.16 fixed point with the step taken by `floor` (a toward-minus-infinity shift), the ends truncated, and the pixel rounded half up: a tie such as a constant 127.5 row rounds up while a drifting row loses a fraction to the floored step, which is why ties looked like they round toward the centre colour in an earlier fit. (4) Alpha and the premultiplied channels interpolate together (translucent captures: 2,180 of 2,181 exact against 2 of 2,181 for straight-alpha interpolation). (5) Device vertices reach the scan converter rounded UP to 1/16 pixel (`ceil(16 v) / 16`; rounding to nearest, truncation and floor all lose 10-20 points of exactness on scaled and rotated captures; quarter turns, 0.75 and 0.6 scales and 10, 25, 30 and 90 degree rotations are the evidence). (6) FocusScales do nothing when the surrounds differ per vertex: 40 captures with isotropic (0.5, 0.5) and anisotropic (0.25, 0.75) scales are 98.7% exact (14,577 of 14,766, none beyond a level) from the plain fan, against 0.5% for the previous model, and the differing-pixel ceilings of the 240 alpha controls (80 of them with a focus) fall from 255,177 to 33,604 in total. Measured on 320 captures (`path-gradient-vertices.json.gz`, `src/emf-plus-path-gradient-vertices.fixture.test.ts`): 120 random polygons on a 1/16 grid 98.78% exact (44,454 pixels, none beyond one level; 0.83% before), 60 translucent ones 99.31% (0.08% before), the four RGB triangles (black and white centres, scales 4 and 0.5) 99.7% (29% before), eight triangle orientations 94.2% (22% before), the single-channel set 98.9% (52% before), quad 97.3% and pentagon 97.7% (11% and 21% before), scaled and rotated sets 95.8-99.0% (0.2-1.0% before); every pixel of these is within one level. `grad-path-triangle-clamp` falls from 1,421 to 13 differing pixels at zero tolerance (none beyond a level), the tiled triangles from about 6,000 (38%) to 70-72 (0.45%); All 120 per-vertex captures of the 240 alpha controls tighten.

Rotated, sheared and anisotropically scaled uniform path gradients take their step count from the UNtransformed bounds: `N = ceil(|M(w, h)| + |M(w, -h)|)` for the brush-space bounds `w` x `h` and the linear part `M` of the brush-to-device matrix (the sum of the two device diagonals of the bounds; it is `ceil(2 * hypot(w, h))` for an unrotated scale, and a rotation does not change it). `PathGradientRotateProbe.cs` captured 70 gradients (a rectangle, a triangle and a wide rectangle, centre off the middle, fractional translation) as world and as brush transforms (the two give identical pixels): rotations of 0 to 200 degrees, a 1.5 x 0.7 scale, a 30-degree rotation of a 2 x 1 scale, shears of 0.25 to 2 in either axis and mixed matrices. Scoring every step count from 60 to 700 against each capture put the best count at the formula's in all 70 (no other count within 12 of it scored as well); the device bounding box of the rotated shape (which grows with the angle, 194 against 145 for a 60 x 40 rectangle at 30 degrees) scored 61% over the first 48 captures, the untransformed bounds 74%, the column norms of `M` 84% (right for rotation and scale, 3 to 6 steps too many under shear), and the diagonal sum 90%. The rest of the 10% was the rounding of the copies, next paragraph (fractional geometry puts a pixel within a few hundredths of a step of a copy edge often: an unrotated rectangle scored 95%). The step count was the only thing wrong with rotation: the nested copies are scaled about the centre in brush space and transformed, which the sampler already did.

Which copy holds a pixel is decided by the geometry GDI+ actually scan-converts, and that closed the ties and near-ties the ratio model rounded the wrong way (`emf-plus-path-gradient-copies.ts`, `pathGradientStepColor`). (1) The boundary's vertices and the centre are rounded to the nearest 1/16 pixel in device space (28.4 fixed point) before anything is scaled; copy `m` is then the exact scale of those points about the rounded centre by `(m + 1/2) / N`, with no rounding of its own, and a pixel takes the innermost copy holding it (an isotropic focus `f` scales a copy by `f + (1 - f)(m + 1/2)/N`, independent focus scales do it per axis). Scoring four fractional-geometry captures (a rectangle and a triangle, each at two rotations, 7,423 pixels): rounding the points to nearest and scaling exactly 100.00%; rounding nothing 91.1%; rounding the copies as well to 1/16 82%, to 1/256 91%; rounding the original points up 81% or down 77%. Over the 46 world-transform captures of the rotated set (107,809 pixels) the exact-scale model is 100.00%, where the smooth ratio with the right step count scores about 90%. (2) A step's colour rounds a value exactly half way between two levels toward the centre colour: 59% of the pixels of an opaque capture with a small colour range (alpha control 236: red 173 to 155, green 43 to 125, blue 6 to 123 over 164 steps) were on the ramp with half-up rounding and 99.93% with the tie rule; 186 of the 240 alpha controls and 20 of the 120 colour controls tighten, and the sum of the 240 alpha differing-pixel ceilings falls from 255,177 to 1,389, and a wider tie margin (1e-4 to 1e-2 of a level) scores worse, so the 0.01-0.02 margin the step-count round measured was the geometry's near-ties. (3) Independent focus scales are the same copies scaled per axis: ten scale pairs on a rectangle (100% of 100,000 pixels in step-reading captures, 99.93% by colour: the rest are one-level ties of a fully focused axis), on a diamond (98.9%) and on a triangle (99.6%), and 40 random triangles and quads (99.2%); the focus-contour captures' anisotropic exact pixels rise from 1,594,271 to 1,725,250 of 1,728,000 with every per-capture ceiling held or lowered, `grad-path-focus-*` rectangles with scales 75/25, 25/75, 50/0 and 0/50 are pixel-exact, `grad-path-ellipse-clamp` falls from 14 differing pixels to none and `gpx-pen-pathgrad` from 48 to 2.

Hypotheses rejected, with evidence: (1) a function of the ratio alone quantised to a fixed number of steps, or to any 2^k, in any rounding (rows of equal width and different height differ); (2) a fixed-point linear ramp with a rounded slope and offset per row (no linear fit exists for either half); (3) the linear-gradient knot table with 8- or 16-bit interpolation (best 259 misses of 8,677 values against 259 for plain rounding); (4) the vertex-distance quantisation as a scale `L / Lq` on the ratio (no better than plain rounding); (5) a 16- to 24-bit fixed-point weight or reciprocal for the sub-level residual below (best 405 misses of 138,188 left-half values against 637 for exact arithmetic); (6) float32 barycentric or edge-DDA arithmetic, edge left/right inclusion and a dx-sign rule for the exact half-step ties (each fits some of the 493 tie pixels and none all: the same geometry in the opposite vertex order flips about half of them); (7) whole-triangle Gouraud or fan-apex Gouraud models for per-vertex surrounds as exact barycentric fills (50% of pixels and errors up to four levels): the fan model was right, the missing piece was the scan converter (see above). (8) rounding the copies to 1/16 or a finer grid, or rounding the original points up or down, and float32 evaluation of the copy scale or of the vertex arithmetic in any of ten orders (see above; none changes the integer-sized ties); (9) a wider tie margin for the colour rounding; (10) a step count from the device bounding box of a rotated shape, from the untransformed bounds, from the column norms of the matrix or from its determinant (61%, 74%, 84% and 75% against 90% for the diagonal sum on the rotated captures, before the copies model took the set to 100%).

Still open, in order of size: (a) pixels exactly on a copy's edge are not decided by a rule: a pixel on an edge of an integer-sized shape (a copy at half scale of a 60 x 40 rectangle has its top edge on a pixel row) is inside the copy at some positions along the edge and outside at others, and the pixel keeps the half-way colour of the two steps, within a level of either answer. ## GDI color adjustment and HALFTONE

- **Colour adjustment** is reproduced from native colour cubes: curve stages (gamma, reference black/white, contrast, brightness, log, negative) are exact, illuminants 1-5, 7 and 8 use native cubes, and colorfulness/tint match the native cubes on 99.5-99.8% of channels (the chroma stage reads the unrounded palette level and rounds before the curves). Windows' 66x65 ordered dither over 32 levels per channel is reproduced, including mirrored destinations, brush origins, `StretchDIBits`, axis-aligned `PlgBlt` and the mixed-axis phase rule; rotated blits follow the session's first-HALFTONE-blit rule. The turned u' is floored at 0, which makes strongly rotated blues (tint beyond about 50) match. The matrix is the one derived from the BT.709 primaries and D65 white (7 decimals), which removed most exact-half ties (0.05-0.4% of channels still differ by one level), and an illuminant combined with colorfulness/tint now carries the illuminant's unclamped linear output (`ILLUMINANT_MATRICES`) into the chroma stage. Remaining: a few strongly blue colours under illuminant + chroma (the illuminant's blue corner is not linear), and a few mixed-axis pixels under colour adjustment (`emfrec-halftone-ramp-0p5x-2y-ca` 0.111%, `-2x-0p5y-ca` 0.07%). When columns are reduced and rows enlarged under a dithered adjustment, Windows sharpens the last row against a further row that is the last source row replicated and dithered at the next pattern row. Reducing both axes (also `StretchDIBits`) is now exact: rows rounded to integers first, columns unrounded, sharpen on the unrounded image.
- **Mixed-axis stretching:** all 96 native size/pattern combinations are byte-exact (single-row destinations and the exact-tie checker patterns are closed: the reduction uses 13-bit cumulative shares per footprint, and a 2-row source squeezed to 1 row is despeckled and, with at least 2x enlargement, reduced to its last row; the enlargement weights are whole 13-bit shares built by cumulative rounding up, and a mirrored enlarged axis reverses the finished rows). Unresolved: the generating formula of the enlargement kernel (`halftoneEnlargeTaps`). Black-box extraction of the integer weight rows (random-data capture of the single-axis response, solved to exact integers) for about 410 size pairs shows the structure (area resample, destination-space FIR sampling one kernel at `k / ratio`, cumulative round-up shares), but no closed form fits it: box-plus-triangle with a free tail, polynomial and spline families of up to 40 parameters, quantised sampling positions and cascaded box filters were all tried and rejected, and only a 96-step interpolation table solved against the measured rows reproduces them (about 98% of the rows it was solved on, about 95% of the rows of sizes held out of the solve). Far taps (a source pixel 1.4-1.5 pixels from the destination centre) also deviate by a share or two in a few rows for ratios just above an integer, which the table cannot absorb. Further native size pairs would tighten the table; the exact formula would remove it.
- **Enlargement pre-smoothing (despeckle):** measured from native captures and now exact on every black/white 4x4 pattern (also at the image edges), every pattern up to 5x3, and several hundred random black/white, grey, palette and noise images from 2x2 to 40x40 at 2x, 3x and 4x/1x2 ratios (`src/halftone-despeckle.fixture.test.ts`). Unverified: brightness ties between the two diagonals of a block beyond the single measured case, the exact brightness weights (4R + 8G + B fits every sample but is a fit), and patterns larger than the sizes above that mix many overlapping checker runs.

- Arc, Chord and Pie geometry follows native `GetPath` captures (`arc-path-probe`: 900 arcs under both arc directions, `arc-paths.json` and `arc-paths-cw.json`, and 260 arcs on a 40,000 px circle, `arc-precise.json`, where one FIX is 3 millionths of the radius). The model, which reproduces 898 of 900 counter-clockwise and 898 of 900 clockwise paths exactly (the other four one FIX off in one point; before: 662 and 234 one FIX off, 4 up to 5 FIX), and the 260 large-circle arcs to within 3 FIX at a radius of 320,000 FIX:
  - An arc radial's angle is Windows' own approximate arc tangent: in each octant the slope (smaller over larger component) goes through 32 equal slope intervals with linear interpolation of the exact arc tangent at the knots, so the angle sits up to 80 microradians below the true one (a sawtooth every 1/32 of the slope; measured to 2 microradians). The direction is the radial over the box's half axes, measured on the unrounded logical coordinates (not on device-rounded points).
  - Points are those of a regular 128-gon inscribed in the unit circle (`polygonTrig`), linear in the angle between its vertices, so they sit up to `1 - cos(pi / 128)` inside the circle (the old table of 33 fitted node values was this polygon plus the arc-tangent error, mistaken for node errors). Arcs whose two radials are 3 degrees apart or less as angles in `[0, 2 pi)` (not across the 0 axis, and not the same radial twice) use exact cosines and sines for their points, but not for the control distance.
  - Each piece (the arc cut at the quadrant boundaries) is a cubic with end points `P0`, `P3` (the polygon points) whose controls divide `P -> X` at `kappa = 4/3 c / (1 + c)`, `c` being the polygon cosine of half the piece's sweep and `X` the intersection of the lines `x . P0 = 1` and `x . P3 = 1` (each a sag outside the true tangent). This single rule replaces the earlier anchored-piece and quadrant-interior models.
  - A whole quadrant between other pieces takes the full ellipse's own Bezier, and so does the first piece of an arc starting on 90 or 180 degrees (270 and 0 mirrored for a clockwise arc, which is the y mirror image) and the last piece of one ending on 270 or 360 (90 and 180 clockwise); a whole-quadrant first or last piece of the other kind, and an arc that is just that one quadrant, is built like any partial piece (as if a zero-length piece came first or last).
  - Arcs are measured as before for the radials, and a RoundRect scales its corner onto the drawn box from the unrounded device size: fractional extents under a cosmetic pen, whole FIX (truncated) under wide and null pens, replacing the earlier even-pixel rule, which matched only the 12 x 9 corner of `wmf-shapes` (`wmf-roundrect-corners`: 75 wide-pen and 25 null-pen corners at two scales; mismatches 500 and 813 pixels to 0 and 0). wmf-insideframe-curved-chord/pie/arc and the inside-frame Chord/Pie/Arc pixels are exact, and `wmf-shapes-scaled` fell from 0.158% to 0.008% (7 pixels).
  - `AngleArc` takes its angles directly (no radial, no arc tangent): the same polygon trigonometry and piece rule, with exact trigonometry for sweeps of 3 degrees or less, now give 599 of 600 native paths exactly (`angle-arc-paths.json`, in the `arc-path-probe` group; before: 71, with 86 having a different number of Beziers). An arc of less than 90 degrees is a single Bezier even across a multiple of 90 degrees (the earlier rule cut it there); from 90 degrees on it is cut at the multiples of 90 and ends with a zero-length piece on a boundary, as before.
  - Not reproduced: four paths with one control or end point one FIX off (arcs close to a full turn or to the 3 degree threshold, where the exact value is within 0.15 FIX of a rounding boundary), arcs of under 1 degree (a native point is up to 2 FIX away at a radius of 320,000 FIX, from fixed-point arithmetic that is not modelled), and the zero-length leading or trailing Bezier Windows emits where an arc starts or ends exactly on a boundary (dropped by the comparison).
- Flat-capped round joins no longer include a vertex that lies exactly on the second ray, and keep a steep segment's extreme-x vertex on the left side (flat/round 5,999 random paths: 86 differing to 1). A wide pen on a curve is widened with per-segment curve rules: the pen rests on a flattened curve's support vertices and round joins, a joint between two cubics takes the tangent of the cubic with the longer control arm, and a curve end's perpendicular and square-cap extension follow its end tangent. `curve-widen-probe` captures 320 native `WidenPath` outlines (80 Beziers, 80 each of arcs, chords and pies; flat, square and round caps; round, bevel and miter joins): the Beziers, all 80 arcs and all 80 pies are exact, and 79 of 80 chords (one outline differs, 1 pixel; previously 148 of 240 arc/chord/pie outlines in 1,437). ## Tooling

- **Regenerating existing groups:** running existing fixture groups on the Windows runner can change their PNGs, because the runner's OS, fonts and DPI can differ. Compare before replacing committed fixtures.

Every new generator run writes an `environment-<groups>.json` manifest associating reference hashes with Windows/native-library versions, screen DPI and installed font hashes. This makes subsequent environment changes detectable; it cannot recover the environment of earlier captures.
