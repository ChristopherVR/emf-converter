# Parity notes

Measured detail behind [Limitations](./limitations.md): the native mechanisms each area follows, the captures that pin them, and the residuals still measured. Open work is in [Outstanding work](./outstanding-work.md); the round-by-round record is the [Parity log](./parity-log.md).

Output is compared with images rendered by Windows. This page describes the current rendering behaviour and known differences from native Windows playback. The exact per-fixture bounds and tolerance levels are defined in `src/gdi-parity.fixture.test.ts`.

Parity is measured by comparing rendered output pixel-for-pixel against images captured from Windows GDI and GDI+. Each case in `src/gdi-parity.fixture.test.ts` carries a single mismatch tolerance bound, many of them nonzero to allow for fixed-point rounding and platform differences. The report script `bun scripts/gdi-fixtures/report.ts` reports zero-tolerance differences separately. See [the current parity priorities](./parity-log.md#current-parity-priorities-8-october-2026) for the zero-tolerance review, text coverage gaps and reference-environment issues.

Single-line EMF+ text alignment now follows native horizontal/vertical layout for supplied fonts with unrotated AntiAlias rendering under positive uniform scales and whole-device-pixel em sizes, including trailing-space measurement. Default italic black-box fitting, grid-fitted, rotated or nonuniformly scaled alignment, fractional device em sizes, wrapping, trimming and complex shaping remain unresolved. The [latest native text controls](./native-parity-review.md#alignment-and-collapsed-monochrome-boxes) distinguish exact output from bounded residuals and record the completed collapsed monochrome-box fix.

## Color adjustment and image effects

- The `HALFTONE` stretch mode matches the existing uniform-axis controls for 32-bit output, but Windows takes a second, filtered enlargement branch for some sources. Which branch is a rule of the source rectangle alone (never the destination size, which the 2x and 3x controls vary, the colour adjustment or the blit API) found with boundary sweeps against the native engine (`src/emf-gdi-halftone-branch.ts`, `halftone-boundary.fixture.test.ts`): the engine counts distinct colours while scanning the rows and compares the count with limits that depend on the rectangle's size. A colour is the exact 24-bit value, except that colours whose red equals blue are identified by the top six bits of blue and green. Up to 2,304 pixels the branch is always replicated; above 16,384 pixels only rows 0, 6, 12, ... are looked at and 20 distinct colours filter; in between every row is scanned, 145 + floor(o / 16) colours filter (o is how far the least row count with more than 2,304 pixels overshoots 2,304), and so do 20 colours with more than 2,304 pixels in rows that introduced a colour, unless duplicate rows first use up a budget. The rule agrees with every one of the 29,763 swept native sources, with all 242 captured 2x sources and with the single-pixel swap that flips a 256 x 16 band source from replicated to filtered. A source whose height is exactly the least row count past 2,304 pixels (8,766 swept sources, 4,500 of them drawn after the rule for that height was found) needs 289 + floor(o / 8) colours or the 20-colour rule, and the scan gives up as soon as the colours counted plus one row of pixels for every duplicate row reach that limit minus one: one duplicate row before the last colours that are needed already ends it when the rows are wide, while rows that each add only a few colours (2 or 5 per row of a 100 x 24 image) never use the budget and filter at the last row. The filtered branch is reproduced for: enlargements of both axes by up to 5x, whole or fractional (the source is sharpened with the four-neighbour kernel and interpolated vertical first with an 8-bit intermediate rounded half up, a replicated and separately sharpened row beyond the top and bottom; each destination pixel belongs to the source pixel under its centre, the destination pixels of one source pixel form a run, and the three weights come from the run's length and the pixel's place in it: a lone pixel `(5,22,5)/32`, a pair `(4,12,0)` and `(0,12,4)` over 16, a run of three `(6,10,0), (1,14,1), (0,10,6)`, four `(6,10,0), (3,12,1), (1,12,3), (0,10,6)` and five `(13,19,0), (6,25,1), (3,26,3), (1,25,6), (0,19,13)` over 32, so a whole factor, 1.5x and 1.025x all draw on the same five sets); either axis enlarged by more than 5x, even by a fraction (horizontal first, the sharpened rows clamped at the edges, the 13-bit shares of the kernel described below, exact for every whole factor from 6 to 16); one axis enlarged while the other keeps its size (only that axis is sharpened, by a one-dimensional pass `v + floor((2v - l - r) / 4)`, and interpolated with the same weights, an exact 2x being linear; exact from 1.025x to 10x); mirrored blits (the mirror image of the unmirrored result); and a combined (dithered) colour adjustment (the source and its extension rows are dithered with the ordered pattern and mapped first; for whole factors up to 5x the pattern runs two rows further down than for the replicated branch, and two rows less far when mirrored vertically). All 240 selection controls, the 80 native 2x captures, all 48 cases of `halftone-filtered` and the one-axis profiles of every phase of every whole factor from 2 to 16 on both axes are pixel-exact; the ten dithered cases among them are exact only since round 6 (the source is padded with each edge row replicated twice and dithered, and the chroma stage's measured tie-breaking is applied for the settings in `emf-gdi-chroma-tie-data.ts`; other colorfulness / tint settings keep a one-level residual in about 0.2% of the channel values). Reducing one axis while the other keeps its size reduces with the same 13-bit shares and sharpens along that axis only (`floor(v + (2v - l - r) / 4)` on the unrounded reduction, edge pixels standing in for missing neighbours; so a row of 127.5 gives 127, not 128), and despeckles first under the reduction rule below; 150 native stretches of every source kind, size band and mirroring are exact (before, none of the random-data ones were: 99% of the values differed). Under a gamma or log curve the curve is applied to the result (22 of 24 captured, the other two 9 and 15 values off by two levels at most); under a dithered colour adjustment a reduction is dithered and mapped at destination coordinates (pattern unmirrored), and an enlargement dithers and maps the source at its own coordinates (a vertical one sharpens its last row against one further dithered row, a mirrored one counts its pattern rows from the far end) before the one-axis filter: 22 and 23 of the 24 captures of each adjustment are exact (the other two differ by one level in 24 and 4 values), where only two of each were before the chroma ties were applied. A mixed enlarge-and-reduce stretch under a dithered adjustment dithers and maps the source (at its own coordinates) when the destination has at least as many pixels as the source, equal areas included, and the finished output (at destination coordinates) when it has fewer, whatever the factors: 96 captured stretches of every kind with every mirroring are exact (a mirrored vertical enlargement with a reduced width sharpens its last row against one further dithered row at pattern row H - 1 - SH too), where the older even-count rule left 32% to 45% of the values of mixed stretches wrong by up to 200 levels. A mixed enlarge-and-reduce stretch (one axis grows, the other shrinks) runs the reduce, sharpen, enlarge sequence below not only for the reduction by 2x or more that the first captures covered but whenever the shrinking axis loses at least 1.5 source pixels per destination pixel (counted `3 * dst + 2 <= 2 * src`: 64 to 42 qualifies, 64 to 43 does not, 100 to 66 qualifies, 50 to 33 does not), whenever the source takes the filtered branch, and whenever the destination has fewer pixels than the source (strictly: equal areas do not; a source of up to 2,304 pixels is despeckled first in that last case). Milder reductions of a replicated-branch source pick single source pixels. The despeckle itself is a step only for sources of up to 2,304 pixels and of more than 16,384; sources in between that take the replicated branch are copied without it (the engine's scan hands over to a plain copy). A reduction of both axes follows the same bands: it despeckles first when the source takes the replicated branch (always up to 2,304 pixels, by the scan above 16,384, and never in between) and neither axis loses 1.5 source pixels per destination pixel (a reduction by 2x, as in the checker fixtures, does not); 120 captured reductions of checker-dense, 3-colour and 27-colour sources across the three size bands are exact (98 before), as are all but one of 700 further random ones. All 1,101 native stretches of `halftone-mixed-engine` that probe these rules are pinned: the 343 engine-grid, 1.5x-boundary and size-band captures, the 120 reductions, 156 of the 160 area-boundary and 154 of the 160 random stretches are exact, the other ten differing by one level in 24 and 13 channel values (float-level boundaries of the enlargement kernel). Stretches at 1.5x, 2.37x, 3.2x by 1.7x, 5.5x and 6.5x by 2.3x of 354 and 171 sources drawn from all twelve groups of the selection sweeps (`halftone-boundary`) take the branch `halftoneBranch` predicts in every case (one of the 525 differs by 17 of 219,384 values). The replicated branch first smooths exact two-by-two checkers, then takes the nearest source pixel (which diagonal of a checker block is pulled toward its surroundings is decided by brightness 4R + 8G + B, a tie taking the top-left/bottom-right pair: 6,400 native blocks and 500 checker-dense images agree exactly, `src/halftone-despeckle-sweep.fixture.test.ts`). A reduction averages each pixel's footprint and then sharpens. All seven plain uniform-axis fixtures (2x, 3x, 0.5x, 0.65x and 1.37x) match exactly. Mixed-axis stretching follows the native reduce, per-axis sharpen, then enlarge sequence; all 96 native size/pattern combinations match byte for byte (`src/halftone-mixed.fixture.test.ts`). The reduction keeps its weights through the sharpening: each footprint is split into 8192 shares, and a source pixel takes the difference of the cumulative shares `floor(j * 8192 / footprint)` at its edges (exact for the native impulse and random-data captures of over 100 size pairs, and for sources up to 300 pixels), and an enlargement other than exactly 2x area-resamples the sharpened samples to destination size and smooths that row with a destination-space FIR that spans one source pixel either way: its taps sample the kernel described below at `k / ratio` source pixels and are normalised to 1. The native weights are whole 13-bit shares: the cumulative weight of a destination pixel up to source pixel `j` is rounded up, a pixel's weight is the difference of two cumulative shares, and a row sums to exactly 8192 (impulse- and random-data-fitted integer weight rows of about 410 size pairs, ratios 1.05x to 8x, and of 78 further pairs, have this structure, and a mirrored enlarged axis reverses the finished rows instead of the source). The kernel is a closed form, found by solving the integer rows of 78 further size pairs (ratios 1.02x to 16x, 8,159 interior rows, `halftone-kernel-rows`) for the kernel with a linear program, which came out smooth, and fitting the knots: with `x = 1 - u` (u the tap's distance in source pixels), the weight is `x ** (1 / sqrt 2)` before half a pixel, `x` at exactly half a pixel and `x ** sqrt 2` from there to one pixel, so the two halves are power laws with reciprocal exponents meeting at a jump (0.613 against 0.375 either side of half a pixel). It reproduces 8,152 of the 8,159 interior rows (99.91%) and the output of all 110 rows whose destination pixel centre lies exactly on a source pixel's edge or centre (106 of them as solved weights); the earlier 96-step table fitted to a smaller set reproduced 90% of them and 89% of held-out pairs. Six interior misses are one share between two neighbouring source pixels at a boundary that lies within 0.002 of a whole share (the native engine's float arithmetic: of 4,773 labelled near-integer cumulative shares the formula is on the native side for 4,570, every miss within 0.0035 of the integer; rounding the formula's intermediates to float32 at any site or combination of sites, truncating them, or quantising the weights, the cover, the kernel or the tap distance made it worse or no better). The 110 rows whose destination centre is exactly on a source pixel's edge or centre and the centre phase of the factor 15 are not misses: such a row's two halves weigh exactly 4096 shares, so its output cannot tell the tails (or the spare share of the odd sum) apart, and the converter's rows reproduce every raw native value of 38 of them. A pair of rows (columns) squeezed into one while the other axis grows is despeckled first (a pixel of a one-pixel 2 x 2 checker block becomes the rounded mean of its pair) and, when the other axis at least doubles, keeps only its last row (column), which is then enlarged like any other single-row source; this closes every single-row destination in the captures. Native ramp/checker fixtures retain colour and edge differences there (bounds in the parity tests). The horizontal ramp enlargement is within one level everywhere. `StretchBlt` and `StretchDIBits` under HALFTONE write destination alpha 0 on every branch and never let the source alpha change the colours (alpha 255, 0, 128 and random give identical RGB in 20 stretches under four plans, `halftone-alpha.json.gz`, `src/halftone-alpha.fixture.test.ts`): the converter ignores source alpha and writes opaque pixels, as the opaque reference PNGs of the playback captures show, where COLORONCOLOR copies the nearest source alpha.
- `EMR_SETCOLORADJUSTMENT` affects `HALFTONE` `StretchBlt` and `StretchDIBits` calls, as in GDI. The stages, their order and constants were measured on native 32x32x32 colour cubes: per-channel gamma, reference black/white, contrast (`exp(0.0148885 c)`), brightness (`0.95625 b`), log and negative curves match every input level exactly. Illuminants 1-5, 7 and 8 use the native colour cubes; colorfulness and tint scale and turn CIE u'v' chroma exactly (1 + c/100, 0.6 degrees per tint unit) and compress out-of-gamut colours affinely (after flooring the turned u' at 0, which the strongly rotated blues need), with a remaining +-1 rounding difference on about 0.05-0.4% of the channels (for the 11 colorfulness / tint settings measured natively the ties are applied from a table of 1,374 flips, `src/emf-gdi-chroma-tie-data.ts`, and those settings are exact; the BT.709 matrix is the 7-decimal one derived from the primaries and D65 white; a colour adjustment that combines an illuminant with colorfulness or tint feeds the illuminant's unclamped linear output into the chroma stage, which is within a level on most channels but still up to 70 levels off for a few strongly blue colours). Illuminant, colorfulness and tint go through Windows' 32-level palette with its 66x65 ordered dither, reproduced from native captures including the pattern origin, so the illuminant controls and the combined-adjustment fixtures are pixel-exact apart from that chroma rounding (at most 3 levels) and, for mixed-axis stretches of non-2x ratios, the geometry bounds below. Native captures cover mirrored enlargement, brush origins and rotated/skewed blits; rotated adjustment follows the first HALFTONE blit in the playback session. Direct-DIB colour-band transitions follow the same branch selection (the 144 `halftone-transitions` captures, see the parity priorities).
- EMF+ image effects (`SerializableObject`) are applied to the image's pixels before it is drawn. MS-EMFPLUS does not specify the algorithms, so they were measured against the effected bitmaps GDI+ itself writes into its recordings (`src/emf-plus-image-effects.fixture.test.ts`). Color matrix, lookup table, brightness/contrast and color balance match the native fixtures exactly. Sharpen strength matches all integer amounts 0–100 independently of radius. Blur: every filter product is kept to 1/256 of a level, the centre weight is applied as two half-weight products that are each rounded on their own, and each sum rounds half up. Below radius 20 GDI+ filters each row horizontally, then vertically filters the leading ceil(height / width) rows, and the converter follows that rule; the native ramp and colour-impulse dimension sweeps are exact. At radius 20 and above, native reduction factors are measured across 957 quarter radii, and the native algorithm is reproduced axis by axis: every row is reduced horizontally (floor of the block mean, a partial last block averaged over its own pixels), filtered at the reduced radius, and enlarged back to full width (linear, truncating, continuing the end line beyond the outermost samples; a single reduced sample leaves one trailing pixel unfiltered); every column of those rows then goes through the same reduce, filter and enlarge. A kernel whose half-width, rounded up to odd, reaches the reduced size (partial block included) clamps at the ends, a narrower one reflects. The kernel weights are float32 values formed in arithmetic that truncates toward zero (the offset accumulates as `q_i = q_(i-1) + 1.4 / radius`, `w_i = exp(-q_i^2)`, the sum is `w_0 + 2 w_1 + 2 w_2 + ...` in order, and each weight is `w_i * (256 / sum)`), which decides every product that lands within a few ten-thousandths of a rounding tie. With them, blur is exact: no pixel differs from GDI+ across random noise images at random radii from 1 to 255 and sizes from 8 to 512 pixels, nor in the ramp, impulse, 957-radius and two-pixel-wide captures, and so is sharpen. Expanded blur reduces a cropped source rectangle from its own corner and enlarges only that rectangle's own reduced samples (the transparent padding samples shape the filtering alone), with samples beyond the buffer transparent rather than reflected; a lone reduced sample is continued toward a transparent neighbour from its block's middle, or from just past the last pixel of a shorter block. The 504 expanded draws and a further 150 narrow and edge-reaching draws are exact. Color curves use complete native lookups for all eight adjustments and every legal intensity, matching all 256 levels. Levels also handles inverted and equal thresholds and the full midtone range; its tables are formed in float32 arithmetic that truncates toward zero and match a 258,560-value sweep and 2.6 million further native values exactly. Hue/saturation/lightness is exact: GDI+ works in all-integer HSL (lightness `(max+min)>>1`, saturation floored to 0-255, a 255-step hue, 16.16 fixed-point controls) and the converter reproduces it for the whole 16.7 million-colour RGB cube at 20 settings and the 1,390,080-pair sweep. Tint is exact: GDI+ works in integer luma-preserving arithmetic (luma weights 54/183/19 over 256, the tint colour from the 256-index hue palette with a maximum of 254, the amount as a signed weight `round(2.55 |amount|)` that scales the largest channel by `w / 256`) and the converter reproduces it for the whole 16.7 million-colour RGB cube at every amount and 20 hues, and the 253,440-pair sweep. Red-eye correction follows a model measured from GDI+'s outputs alone (a clean-room, black-box fit; see the header of `src/emf-plus-image-effects.ts`): per area, redness `R - max(G, B)` is pulled toward grey in 60 six-degree sectors around a centroid of weights `redness / L`, where `L = round((9 G + 2 B) / 11)` is an integer luma (red plays no part; the weight is 1 when `L = 0`, from 2% to 75% of the red pixels at luma 0, `redeye-zero-fraction`). The centre, radius, falloff and sector means are formed in float32 arithmetic that truncates toward zero (the arithmetic of the blur kernel and the levels tables): centroid sums row by row over pixel indices with half a pixel added to the quotients, a truncated square root for the distance. This replaced the special cases for 'symmetric' scenes (previous weights `G + 0.225 B`, a 0.81 carry, a float32 exactness test), which approximated the same rounding noise: with it 77 of 77 nudged 31 x 31 scenes (`redeye-nudge`, from 59), 191 of 192 independent fresh-process controls (`redeye-fresh`, from 170; 167 of the 168 with non-zero blue) and 72 of 72 held-out ones (from 71) are exact. A pixel is counted in its sector when its squared distance is below the squared radius and corrected when its distance is below the radius; the two differ for a pixel exactly on the circle in exact arithmetic (the end of a centred axis), which is corrected but not counted. The previous area's centroid, in pixel-index coordinates and before any fallback, is added once to the next area's weighted sums (an area without red passes it on, a red one chains it). A centroid farther from the area's middle than `(w + h) / 6` is replaced by the centroid of the darkness `255 - L` over every pixel of the area, red or not (a uniform area gives its middle; a red band at the top of a field pulls the centre toward it by its share of the weight), and the falloff then is at least `0.33 (1 - u)`, linear in the distance, whatever the strength (the 709 lone pixels beyond the circle of the stage captures are exact with a slope between 0.329 and 0.3325, from 571 with the quadratic falloff alone, and 72 more on 44 x 36 fields of luma spread 0, 30, 60 and 100 are exact with 0.33, `redeye-fallback-strength`). The stand-in centre is a measurement, not a fit: the native output of the 256 x 64 ramps image of the fixtures is reproduced exactly only by centres inside a box 0.003 pixel wide (127.641 to 127.644 by 30.564 to 30.567) and the darkness centroid lands in it (127.6429, 30.5660; the middle, (128, 32), leaves 362 pixels); pure red ramps in the top rows of grey fields of levels 0 to 150 and band heights 4 to 24 follow it too (`redeye-fallback-centre`: 23 of 28 captures exact, from 8). The pixels exactly on the horizontal axis through the centroid (both sides and the centre pixel) are one sector group with the 0 to 6 degree sector: the left axis counts as angle 0 rather than 180. Each sector's mean redness sets the correction through a quadratic falloff whose strength steps from 1/4 to 1/2, 0.66 and 3/4 when the area's 30th-to-70th-percentile luma spread exceeds 25, 40 and 80 (0.661 before the float32 arithmetic; 0.66 makes all 30 two-valued fields exact); a second term `(1 - D / Dm)^2 / 2` with the darkness `D = G + 2/9 B` corrects pixels much darker than their sector (pupils) and is used below twice the sector mean. The one brightest pixel (its luma strictly above every other pixel's and at least 40 above the 70th percentile) is corrected completely, and no pixel near it keeps more than five levels of redness per squared pixel of distance, provided it lies inside the corrected disc (outside it the area has no highlight). Output is `R -= round(11/16 a)`, `G, B += round(5/16 a)`, rounded half up on the final value. The native fixtures are exact: 0 mismatching pixels in `plus-only-effect-redeye-left`, `-both` and `-whole` (from 3, 59 and 379; the 282 + 57 pixels of the pure-red ramp band, the 80 of the (x, 255 - x, 128) band and the 17 + 3 skin-tone pixels had three causes: the centre of the far-off centroid, the radius that follows from it, and the float32 truncation). The stage captures (`redeye-stages`, `src/emf-plus-redeye-stages.fixture.test.ts`) pin the rules one at a time: all 412 lone red pixels inside the fallback circle and all 709 beyond it are exact, as are 120 of 120 centroid-weight captures, 13 of 14 carry captures, 30 of 30 strength fields and 81 of 81 highlight captures. Held-out scenes (`redeye-scenes`: 36 seeded smooth fields with one to three red clusters, one fresh process each, captured after the model was fixed) are exact in 30 (17 with the previous model). Process state is not what left the fixture residuals: native playback of the recordings is the committed reference in every fresh process whatever came before it (`redeye-playback`: 17 contexts, 32 playbacks, 0 differing pixels), and the effect applied to the generator's own eyes and ramps images in a fresh process is the same bitmap (`redeye-fixture-direct`, 8 of 8 exact). Not reproduced by a stateless converter: a direct `GdipBitmapApplyEffect` call that follows a two-valued field of 16 x 16 pixels or more in the same process and holds red pixels of luma 0 with no other spread information (a pure-red area) takes its strength from stale memory of the earlier call. The same sequence run in separate fresh Windows processes (`redeye-state`: 30 sequences, 6 processes each) gives different outputs for 7 sequences: a pure-red 24 x 24 area after a spread-120 field removes 91 levels of red at its middle pixel in five processes and 30 in the sixth, and the first call of a process is exact in 180 of 180 captures (a pure-red area gets strength 1/4), so the model is what a fresh process does. Also not reproduced: areas whose red centroid is far from the middle in bright fields (grey fields from level 151 correct nothing at 128 x 128 and another set of 207 pixels at 256 x 64; six of the 36 held-out scenes, 8 to 952 pixels off: the stand-in centre does not explain them), and rounding-sized pixels (one in a noise-field scene, 4 and 1 pixels of black fields of 128 and 160, one carry-capture pixel whose value lands 8e-6 above a half, and the 75%-luma-0 noise field of `redeye-zero-fraction`, 75 pixels: 38 of 40 captures exact, from 30).
- EMF+ image effects run on the premultiplied playback bitmap. A recorded effect (the baked bitmaps in each Dual fixture) runs on the straight ARGB bitmap, but when GDI+ plays a metafile back each translucent colour has been premultiplied (rounded) and the effect sees it restored by truncation (`floor(round(c a / 255) 255 / a)`); the effect's output is then composited with rounded premultiplication. `EffectAlphaProbe.cs` (`effect-alpha`, `src/emf-plus-effect-alpha.fixture.test.ts`) draws a 256 x 256 grey-level-by-alpha source through nine effects both ways: straight ARGB sources composite exactly like the model in all 1,769,472 values, and premultiplied sources over black agree in 99.0% to 99.5% of the 196,608 values of each effect (every alpha-128 value exact) against 71% to 83% without the step. The restore is exact for the half-transparent bar of the fixtures, and about 1% of the other alphas are one or two levels low where `p 255 / a` is an exact integer (a float rounding that was not identified). This closed the 96 half-transparent pixels, one level off, of 30 `plus-effect-*` / `plus-only-effect-*` fixtures (tint, levels, colour matrix, colour LUT, colour balance, hsl, brightness/contrast, sharpen) and the 8 to 56 pixel blur and sharpen residuals (`blur-r1`, `r2p5`, `r3`, `r10` and their expand variants, `sharpen-r6-a30`): 687 of 870 report comparisons are exact, from 646, and every Only recording but the rotated blur is exact at tolerance 0 (their tolerances were 1 to 3 levels).
- EmfPlusDual recordings add a processed bitmap fallback after the effect draw. EmfPlusOnly recordings carry the effect and original source without that fallback. Both types have been regenerated and tested: 74 cases each, plus an explicit Dual blur case. Only recordings test the effect algorithms directly (red-eye included). Deferred PNG and SVG draws preserve their effects and cropped source mapping.
- An effect is applied to the draw's source rectangle only, reading one more column and row, as GDI+ does. A blur with `expandEdge` blurs transparency in at the edges but, as in GDI+, draws nothing beyond the source rectangle. MS-EMFPLUS requires pixel source units; malformed non-pixel records do not apply effects.
- GDI+ records a color curve effect under the lookup table's identifier, and its own playback then draws the image without the effect. The converter does the same.
- An effect is skipped when the image cannot be decoded to pixels. PNG, BMP, JPEG, GIF and TIFF have bundled decoders. Animated GIF and multipage TIFF initially draw their first frame/page, as GDI+ does. Native references verify offset GIF frames, background colours, transparency, local palettes, LZW-compressed and multipage TIFF, plus odd-width bilevel uncompressed, PackBits and CCITT Group 3/4 TIFF. Group 3 also handles reversed bit order and multiple strips. Deflate strips (both compression tags and horizontal prediction) and uncompressed/Deflate tiles also match native references exactly. JPEG-compressed TIFF strips and tiles (RGB and YCbCr) and standalone JPEG (greyscale, RGB, 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:2:0, progressive) match the native references exactly: the bundled decoder reproduces libjpeg's accurate integer IDCT, triangle chroma upsampling and fixed-point colour conversion. Arithmetic-coded JPEG is decoded by the bundled decoder and matches GDI+ exactly on the libjpeg-turbo sample and on 23 encoder-made native captures (`scripts/gdi-fixtures/arith-jpeg-encoder.ts`, a port of libjpeg's `jcarith.c`): sequential and progressive (spectral selection with and without successive approximation), restart intervals, custom DAC conditioning (including extreme L/U/K values), 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:4:0 and 4:2:0 sampling, greyscale, and two images (the 37 x 19 pattern and a 61 x 45 high-magnitude noise image). GDI+ refuses none of these variants. Arithmetic CMYK and YCCK (sequential, progressive, restarts) decode to the same pixels as a Huffman copy of the same coefficients and go through the CMYK conversion described below. A 12-bit JPEG cannot be decoded by Windows either ("Unsupported JPEG data precision 12"), and GDI+ draws nothing for it; the converter does the same, in PNG and SVG output. CMYK and YCCK JPEG (Adobe transform 0 or 2, or no Adobe marker, whose samples are always read as inverted) are converted the way Windows does it. GDI+ sends them through the Windows ICM colour-management module (`mscms.dll`, the RSWOP CMYK profile to sRGB, perceptual intent, best-quality transform: byte for byte what `TranslateBitmapBits` returns), which resamples the profile onto a 16 x 16 x 16 x 16 table of 16-bit integer colours, interpolates it tetrahedrally (the four fractions sorted in descending order) and keeps `(S + 4096) >> 20` of the weighted node sum `S` (integer weights summing to 4096). Where an ink sits on the grid is the profile's own input curve: the A2B0 tag of RSWOP.icm carries a 256-entry table per ink (the same four times), `t(0) = 0`, `t(v) = 256 v - 1` up to `v = 224`, then a steeper line to `t(255) = 65535` (`cmykInputCurve` in `src/jpeg-cmyk.ts`, checked against all 1,024 table entries by `generate-cmyk-lut.ts curve`). `TranslateColors` accepts 16-bit ink words (a `COLOR` record is 16 bytes in a 64-bit process, 8 in a 32-bit one); for `257 v` it returns the 16-bit colour of the 8-bit ink `v`. The module takes a word `w` to `x = w - (w >> 8) - 2`, steps through the profile table cut into 16 steps per entry (plus one, but for the first entry) with the weight `r / 15` of the low four bits of `x`, and the result is a grid position in whole 1/4096 cells (`15 k + r` below cell 13; `cmykInputPositions`: `240 v - 1` for the 8-bit inks up to 224, a steeper run to the last nodes, `v = 255` exactly on node 15), so the interpolation weights are integers. Found by a sweep of every 16-bit word along the 32 edges of the ink hypercube (`scripts/gdi-fixtures/cmyk-edge-sweep.py`; `icm-cmyk-edges.bin.gz`, `src/icm-cmyk-edges.fixture.test.ts`): blocks of 16 words with weights r / 15, one extra repeated word every 256 words, and a once-rounded line of the interpolated node value that is *not* a straight line of the 16-bit output, because the 16-bit result passes through a 1,025-entry output table, `p = (n 65472 / 65535 + 511/1024) / 64` into `K[m] = (65599 m + 511) >> 10` interpolated linearly, which adds one level every 1,040 levels spread over 64. The 8-bit result does not use that table: it is `(S + 4096) >> 20`, which is why it differs from the top byte of the 16-bit one for 0.08% of values (always where the 16-bit word ends in 0x00 or 0xff). The 65,536 nodes (signed 16-bit integers, nodes beyond the sRGB gamut unclipped) are the module's own: a 16-bit probe placed exactly on every node (the word `w` with position `4096 j` per ink) reads back through the output table to the node value, and the clipped, ambiguous and unreachable read-backs (2,968 of 65,536 nodes) and the last few nodes are refined against 19 million native conversions by `scripts/gdi-fixtures/generate-cmyk-nodes.py` (exact 8-bit interval constraints, heaviest weights first, then the 16-bit conversions for what 8 bits cannot decide; the RSWOP profile itself, which Windows ships, is not bundled). `src/jpeg-cmyk.ts` bundles the table (about 275 KB of text, 79 KB more than the fitted 196 KB one). Every channel of the CMYK and YCCK patch, ramp and photo references is exact (all 8 files, 49,152 or 18,432 channels each), as is the TIFF strip file and the 8,000 committed samples; on 30,000 held-out random inks (`icm-cmyk-val.bin.gz`) none is wrong, and on 10,000,000 fresh random inks (not committed) 43 of 30,000,000 channels differ by one level (the earlier fitted table: 0.26% of them), all at a byte boundary of a node sum, mostly with a node beyond the gamut among its five. CMYK JPEG inside a TIFF (PhotometricInterpretation 5) goes through the same transform with the ink amounts read as stored, not inverted (96 x 64 reference: exact). Chroma subsampling and Adobe/no-Adobe variants behave like the exact decoder; the earlier `jpeg-js` fallback matched 1% to 11% of CMYK channel values with errors up to 135, and the 17^4 grid fitted to GDI+ output before the transform was identified matched 87% to 95% with errors up to 6. Canvas backends are bypassed for these families, so `@napi-rs/canvas` and browsers cannot change the result; in SVG output such files are embedded as decoded pixels rather than as the original JPEG. A canvas backend remains preferred when available. Other encoded formats may need a browser or `@napi-rs/canvas`.
- CMYK 8-bit result: exact for all 2^32 ink combinations. The module's 8-bit routine reads unsigned 16-bit nodes and returns `S >> 20` of the weighted sum (output table entry `S >> 18`); `nodeTable8` in `src/jpeg-cmyk.ts` is the 16-bit-probe table plus one, clamped to 16 bits, with `NODE_DELTA_8BIT`, 89 corrections to interior nodes (47 of -1, 32 of +1, nine of two to five units) found by `scripts/gdi-fixtures/cmyk-8bit-nodes.ts` (exhaustive native sweep, then a boundary-constraint solve, repeated until a sweep finds no wrong ink). The 16-bit probes (`cmykColour16`) read the module's generic interpolator and keep the original table. `icm-cmyk-fit8.bin.gz` and `icm-cmyk-heldout8.bin.gz` pin inks the uncorrected table missed.

## Pen transforms

EMF+ pen transforms (uniform, non-uniform and skewed) match GDI+ exactly on the 17 pen-transform fixtures. That includes dashes, which GDI+ lays out along the path in world space at the untransformed pen width. Native fixtures verify dashes under uniform and non-uniform scales, including antialiasing. GDI+ rejects singular pen transforms; the converter ignores such malformed transform data. Square, round, diamond and arrow anchor caps use their native shapes; the new centered-cap fixtures match every pixel with and without antialiasing.

Clockwise inside-frame RoundRects round vertical inset widths up and curve controls up, with horizontal half-FIX shifts applied in both arc directions. `emf-insideframe-scaled` and `wmf-shapes-scaled` are now pixel-exact. In `GM_ADVANCED` an Arc, Chord or Pie under an inside-frame pen wider than its box paints nothing and has an empty path (as measured with a DIB probe and `GetPath`), where an Ellipse, Rectangle or RoundRect keeps the box as given. 2,400 native `GetPath` captures at fractional scales (`emf-scaled-paths.json.gz`) match every shape, counter-clockwise and clockwise, point for point. A pen exactly as wide as the box leaves a zero-size frame: an Ellipse, Rectangle and RoundRect still match (the corner geometry is scaled onto it; one RoundRect is a FIX off), while Windows builds an Arc, Chord or Pie there from rounding noise, which is not reproduced. In `GM_COMPATIBLE` (WMF) a pen wider than an Ellipse or RoundRect leaves the box without the pen's inset.

## WMF

- `PS_INSIDEFRAME` rectangles, ellipses, rounded rectangles, chords, pies and arcs match the native 0.5–10 px half-pixel sweeps exactly. `wmf-shapes-scaled` (0.96 device scale) is pixel-exact. At that scale an odd device pen width puts a curved shape's vertical edges on half a FIX and shears its points with height; 1,500 native `GetPath` captures (`wmf-scaled-paths.json`) match every Ellipse, Chord, Pie and Arc point for point, and all 148 odd-width RoundRects. A chord whose pen is as wide as its box (one of the 1,500) is a FIX off at four values: Windows still builds an 8 FIX sliver from the inverted box. An Ellipse or RoundRect with such a pen keeps the box without the pen's inset, as reproduced (`wmf-scaled-paths.fixture.test.ts`). RoundRects under cosmetic, wide and null pens match native at the identity and 0.96 scales for every corner size from 6 to 30 units. Arc, Chord and Pie paths follow Windows' angle and trigonometry model (an approximate arc tangent in 32 slope intervals per octant, a 128-gon for the sine and cosine, and tangent lines on the polygon points): of 900 native arcs, 898 paths match exactly under each arc direction and the other two differ by a single 1/16-pixel unit in one point.
- Mirrored `LAYOUT_RTL` cosmetic and wide line fixtures match Windows exactly.
- Metric map modes default to a 96 dpi reference device. Set `wmfReferenceDpi` to a number or `{ x, y }` when the original physical-device resolution is known. Metafiles do not always record that information.
